## What was done

For each of the 20 batch-2 candidates the Content Factory's live pipeline was run:
`retrieveSource` (robots-respecting, polite 1.5s delay) → `researchDraft`
(`verifyClaims` against the retrieved passages) → `buildReviewPack`. A claim is
marked **verified only when an actual passage from the retrieved page overlaps it
at or above the threshold**, and that passage is carried through verbatim as the
excerpt. No citation, quotation, verdict or score was invented. The rubric was
re-run on the real evidence; the **original (no-evidence) score is preserved
alongside the updated one**.

### Supported vs unsupported

- **Supported claims found:** exactly **one** — on *writing-a-cv-that-gets-read*,
  the claim "match your CV to the job" was confirmed by a real passage from the
  National Careers Service page ("You should write your CV to match the job and
  company you're applying for.").
- **Every other claim is unsupported, contradicted, or the source was
  inaccessible.** This is the honest result: the candidates' paraphrased takeaways
  were mostly *not* substantiated by matching passages, and several sources could
  not be retrieved (HTTP errors) or were disallowed by robots.txt (respected).

### Regraded quality

- Original (no-evidence) rubric had 6 `queue` / 14 `reject`. **With real evidence,
  all 20 re-grade to `reject`** — because the accuracy axis now reflects that the
  claims are unsupported by the retrieved text, or the source was inaccessible.
  The gate was **not** forced; the honest consequence of checking is that none of
  these candidates is ready, and the one partial match is not enough on its own.

### High-impact candidates needing additional review

## Review packs

Each candidate's pack (in the JSON) contains the nine required sections:
1. educational objective + topic (and category/level); 2. claim-by-claim evidence
(verdict, overlap, real excerpt when verified, note); 3. source provenance (URL,
publisher, retrieval status, HTTP status, retrieved/source dates, missing-timestamp
flag); 4. evidence gaps & contradictions; 5. original **and** updated rubric
scores; 6. high-impact classification; 7. duplicate screening (against batch 1
**and** batch 2); 8. reviewer questions; 9. recommended next editorial action —
each explicitly labelled **"Not an approval."**

## Honesty boundaries

- Real source **metadata** (URL, publisher, retrieval status, HTTP status) is
  distinct from verified **facts**: only one claim is evidence-supported; the rest
  are not, and are labelled so.
- `robots`-disallowed and HTTP-errored sources are reported as inaccessible — they
  were **not** bypassed and nothing was invented in their place.
- Retrieval used a fixed logical timestamp (2026-11-21) injected into the pipeline;
  the run itself was performed on that date.
